## CSCM445 — Machine Learning

### Official syllabus order

1. Introduction to machine learning and AI
2. K-means clustering — unsupervised method
3. Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) — unsupervised method
4. Linear Regression — supervised method
5. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimension reduction
6. Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) for dimension reduction and classification
7. Neural Networks (NN) — supervised method
8. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) — supervised method
9. Support Vector Machine (SVM) — supervised method
10. Logistic Regression (LR) — supervised method
11. Revision

### Delivery / assessment facts captured from the supplied material

- Taught September–January (TB1)
- Examination, coursework and laboratory work are listed assessment methods
- 40 hours of lectures and labs
- Python is explicitly referenced in the linear-regression learning outcome

### Learning-outcome emphasis

The supplied material requires understanding and application of supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning concepts; clustering; regression; PCA/LDA; neural networks/CNNs; SVM; logistic regression; implementation; evaluation and communication of results.

## Finite Element Method & Structural Dynamics

The module code was not visible in the supplied material. Do not invent one.

### Official prerequisite statement

The supplied syllabus assumes prior knowledge of:

- basic finite elements and dynamics (specifically content comparable to EG-3105);
- basic MATLAB programming.

### Part 1 — Finite element analysis

1. Isoparametric finite elements
2. Numerical integration
3. 2D heat transfer
4. Quadrilateral and high-order elements
5. Mesh generation
6. 2D and 3D elasticity
7. Introduction to non-linear problems

### Part 2 — Dynamic analysis

8. Time integration for transient heat transfer
9. Explicit and implicit time integration
10. Fundamentals of structural dynamics — review of SDOF, MDOF and modal analysis
11. Structural dynamics
12. Newmark method

### Delivery facts captured from the supplied material

- Lectures and computer-based example classes: approximately 2–3 hours/week, total 50 hours
- Directed private study: approximately 3–4 hours/week, total 70 hours
- MATLAB programming is used throughout

### Learning-outcome emphasis

The supplied material requires formulation of FEM models for elasticity and heat conduction; assessment of accuracy/reliability; dynamic analysis under time-dependent loading; resonance/damping/stiffness–mass–damping behaviour; and implementation/evaluation of numerical methods.

## EG-M384 — Engineering Simulation

### Official syllabus order

1. Review of computational modelling and the use of simulation in design
2. Development of computational modelling strategies, including:
   - problem definition;
   - assumptions;
   - simplifications;
   - governing physics;
   - effect of these choices on modelling strategy
3. CAD and meshing considerations and their effect on simulation accuracy
4. Overview of commonly used analysis techniques, including:
   - finite volume methods;
   - finite element methods;
   - advantages, disadvantages and common applications
5. Introduction to modelling tools used for:
   - flow;
   - heat transfer;
   - structural mechanics;
   - interactions between these physics
6. Overview of optimisation tools
7. Case studies to:
   - investigate product performance;
   - optimise product design/implementation against operational criteria

### Delivery / assessment facts captured from the supplied material

- 10 hours lectures
- 30 hours PC laboratory sessions
- Two reports are listed as the assessment method

### Learning-outcome emphasis

The supplied material emphasizes selecting appropriate modelling strategies, understanding modelling limitations, analysing simulation outputs, producing optimized solutions, and communicating a complex engineering problem and solution in a written report.
